01 / RECYCLE WITH CONFIDENCE

Not sure where
it goes?

Recycling rules can differ from place to place. Start with these common items, then check your local council or waste provider for the final word.

▤OFTEN RECYCLABLE

Paper & cardboard

Keep it clean and dry. Greasy or food-soiled bits usually belong in the rubbish or compost.

Quick tip Flatten boxes to save space.
♧OFTEN RECYCLABLE

Glass bottles & jars

Empty and give them a quick rinse. Lids and broken glass may need different handling.

Quick tip Reuse a jar before recycling it.
◉OFTEN RECYCLABLE

Metal cans

Food and drink cans are widely recyclable when empty. Rinse away leftover food.

Quick tip Check whether aerosols are accepted.
♻CHECK LOCALLY

Plastic packaging

Accepted plastics vary. Look for your local recycling symbol guide and keep items empty.

Quick tip Soft plastics may need a store drop-off.
⌁SPECIAL DROP-OFF

Batteries & e-waste

Don't put batteries or electronics in household bins; they can cause fires and need safe handling.

Quick tip Find a dedicated collection point.
✳REUSE FIRST

Clothes & textiles

Repair, swap, or donate wearable pieces. Use a textile collection for worn-out fabric.

Quick tip Keep textiles clean and dry.

No matching items. Try another search.

ⓘ These are general pointers, not universal rules. When in doubt, look up your local recycling service.

02 / LIVE A LITTLE LIGHTER

Less waste.
More life.

Recycling matters, and using less in the first place matters too. Try one small switch that feels right for you.

Find a creative idea ↗
01

Bring your own

Keep a reusable bag or cup where you'll remember it — by the door, in your work bag, or in the car.

↗
02

Make food last

Plan a few meals, store food well, and turn leftovers into tomorrow's lunch or a new dish.

↗
03

Borrow, mend, repeat

Before buying, see if you can borrow it. Repair the things you love and pass along what you don't need.

↗
04

Choose less packaging

When it's practical, buy loose produce, refill a container, or choose products with recyclable packaging.

03 / MAKE SOMETHING NEW

Waste less.
Make more.

A few things that might have been thrown away can become something useful, playful, or simply beautiful.

01
15 MIN · BEGINNER

Jar herb garden

Give an empty glass jar a second life as a windowsill home for fresh herbs.

What you'll need & how to make it

Clean jar, small stones, potting mix, and herb seeds or a small seedling. Add stones for drainage, fill with soil, plant your herb, and place in a bright spot. Avoid leaving excess water in the jar.

02
20 MIN · BEGINNER

Cardboard desk tidy

Turn sturdy packaging into a handy home for pens, notes, and little bits.

What you'll need & how to make it

Clean cardboard tubes or small boxes, scissors, and paper or non-toxic paint. Trim to different heights, arrange on a flat cardboard base, then decorate and let dry. Ask an adult for help with cutting.

03
30 MIN · BEGINNER

T-shirt tote bag

Keep a much-loved tee in use as a lightweight bag for errands or library books.

What you'll need & how to make it

Clean old T-shirt and scissors. Cut off the sleeves and widen the neck opening; cut fringe along the bottom, then tie the matching fringe strips together to close the base. Have an adult help with scissors if needed.
